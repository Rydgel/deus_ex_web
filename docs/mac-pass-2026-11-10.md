## Open and unexplained

* **Chromium `instructor-conversation` (7.1.1, 7.10.2).** Fails 6 of 6 with the spec's default Chromium, which draws with
  SwiftShader on the Mac at 18 fps, and passes 3 of 3 on Metal. The likely cause is the spec's polls straddling a 1.1 to 1.8 s
  greeting through slow round trips. Not checked: what this spec's Chromium draws with on Linux, what stalls the page for about a
  second at the conversation's start, and which other specs draw in software on the Mac. Making the spec robust to a slow page, or
  launching the suite's Chromium on Metal on macOS, is a spec or config change this pass did not make.
* **Firefox `gameplay` (7.1.3).** One failure in the first run, which overlapped this session's own browser launch; a pass alone
  and in the clean rerun. A panel offset read one step short of its key (−55.99994 for −56): a race the spec can lose on a
  disturbed host, likely, not traced.
* **The code discard (7.2.4).** On the Mac every gap over 50 ms opens at a major collection's `prepare.mark_discard_code`, and
  the frames after it run 3 to 10 times slower for 100 to 250 ms. That the slowdown is the scripts running uncompiled until they
  are compiled again is an inference: a markers-only profile has no samples. A sampled profile of the same window (at the cost of
  frame time) would show it; whether Linux's gaps at 39 to 48 s also open at the discard was not checked. Gecko's
  `FULL_GC_TIMER` and `ALLOC_TRIGGER` collections discard code and `CC_FINISHED` ones do not, in every roam here.
* **`gecko-gaps.mjs` on quiet runs.** Its clock search needs page pauses of 15 ms or more; these runs have none, and it reports
  offset −3000 ms and no attribution. The join of `gecko-gc-windows.mjs` (no search) placed every gap within milliseconds of
  its slice. The tool was not changed in this pass.
* **7.5.2 on the Mac.** Play's figures are measured; the original under CrossOver was not captured, so the Mac has no
  original of its own to compare with.
* **Why loads are faster than on 2026-11-09 in both browsers.** The quiet host explains Firefox's outliers; Chromium was 27 %
  faster too, which the host alone may not explain. This pass did not separate the host from the tree (`57f28f9f` against
  `177e242a`).
