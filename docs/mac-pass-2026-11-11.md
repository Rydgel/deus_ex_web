**The repository's reader refuses the Mac's trace.** `node scripts/audio-latency-original.mjs --trace
.work/audio-output/latency-training.txt` stops with `no buffer dword follows A (best b8 178/200)`. It finds Wine's play cursor
as the buffer object's dword that equals Galaxy's cursor A at each callback, and requires that at 90 % of the first 200
callbacks. Here the best field, `+0xb8`, matches 178 (89 %); on Linux `+0xb4` matched 195 of 200. The cause, from the trace:
* **The cursor is out of step with the callbacks.** Under CrossOver it moves in 2,048-byte steps (11.6 ms of audio; every one
  of the first 5,000 steps is 2,048). Galaxy's callbacks advance A by 4,096, 2,048 or 6,144 bytes (62, 31 and 6 % of the first
  5,000).
* **So a poll sometimes reads it one step late.** Over all 58,959 callbacks, `+0xb8` equals A at 90.4 %. At 9.4 % it is
  exactly one 2,048-byte step past A, its previous value equal to A, the step within the same 250 µs poll. At the other 0.3 %
  it is the same one step, but 0.25 to 0.84 ms before the poll.

No other field matches A at all (the next best, `+0x2c`, 0 of 200). So `+0xb8` is Wine's play cursor and the check's 90 % is
what fails. The figures below come from a copy of the script outside Git
